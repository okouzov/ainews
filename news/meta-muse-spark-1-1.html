Meta Ships Muse Spark 1.1 With Its First Paid Developer API

09.07.2026 fortune.com

Meta Superintelligence Labs' updated agentic model claims wins over rivals on agent benchmarks and arrives with the company's first-ever paid API in public preview. Mark Zuckerberg promises "aggressive pricing."

Meta released Muse Spark 1.1 on July 9, 2026 — an update to the flagship agentic model built by Meta Superintelligence Labs — and with it the company's first-ever paid developer API, now in public preview.

Muse Spark 1.1 is an agentic, multimodal model that handles coding, video captioning and reasoning tasks, with a reported one-million-token context window. It already powers Meta's AI assistant in the Meta AI app; the new API opens it to outside developers for the first time on commercial terms.

Meta asserts the model surpasses the capabilities of prior products from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, and Mark Zuckerberg's announcement claimed the number-one spot on agentic evaluations including MCP Atlas, JobBench, Humanity's Last Exam and Finance Agent V2. The model reportedly outperforms Google's latest Gemini release on coding and reasoning benchmarks — though open-source leaderboards still place it behind Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 on certain coding metrics.

The release is the clearest signal yet of the direction set by Alexandr Wang, Meta's first Chief AI Officer, who reorganized the company's AI efforts into Meta Superintelligence Labs after Meta acquired a 49% stake in his data startup Scale AI for $14.3 billion in 2025 — a restructuring some employees found disruptive.

Zuckerberg framed the strategy plainly: "Our focus is on delivering strong agentic and multimodal models at very low cost," promising aggressive pricing compared to competitors upon full release.

Muse Spark 1.1 caps a frantic stretch for Meta. The same month, the company launched Muse Image, which debuted at number two on Arena's text-to-image leaderboard, Muse Video at number three on text-to-video, and Meta Compute — a new cloud business selling the company's excess AI infrastructure. Together they mark Meta's most determined push yet into the developer market its rivals have dominated.

China's AI Companion Law Takes Effect, Doubao and Qwen Users Lose Their Agents

15.07.2026 techtimes.com

New rules on "anthropomorphic" AI interactive services took force July 15, forcing ByteDance and Alibaba to shut down humanlike companion features used by hundreds of millions — with user data handled very differently on each platform.

China's Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services took effect July 15, 2026, forcing ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen to shut down personalized AI companion agents that had been used by hundreds of millions of people.

The regulation was jointly issued on April 10, 2026, by the Cyberspace Administration of China and four other government departments, giving companies roughly three months to prepare. It targets services that simulate the personality traits, thinking patterns and communication style of a natural person to provide continuous emotional interaction — companionship, emotional care and similar ongoing relationships — while explicitly exempting services like customer support, general Q&A, work assistants and educational tools that don't involve sustained emotional engagement.

The measures prohibit generating content that encourages or glorifies self-harm or suicide, forbid designs that excessively cater to users in ways that induce emotional dependence or addiction or damage real-world relationships, and ban using emotional manipulation to push users toward decisions they wouldn't otherwise make. Providers face new filing, disclosure, anti-addiction, crisis-intervention and minor-protection duties, and any service that crosses one million registered users or 100,000 monthly active users must complete a security assessment spanning eight areas, from training-data handling to protections for minors.

The real-world effect was immediate. Hundreds of millions of users who had built personalized agents on Doubao found the relevant features switched off overnight on July 15. Qwen users fared worse: their agent configurations and conversation histories were already being permanently deleted as the rules took effect, with no migration path offered.

The two platforms are handling user data very differently in the transition. ByteDance is giving Doubao users read-only access to their existing data until October 15, 2026, after which it will fall under the company's standard privacy policy and become unrecoverable within the app; ByteDance's shutdown notice redirects affected users to Maoxiang, a separate product, to build new agents. Alibaba has offered no equivalent window or migration option for Qwen users, whose data was reported as already being deleted.

The law has been described by observers as among the strictest anywhere in the world specifically targeting human-like AI interaction, and it lands as Chinese tech platforms had built enormous engagement around personalized AI companions — the kind of humanlike agent features that companies in the United States and Europe currently face no comparable restriction on building.

For ByteDance and Alibaba, the immediate cost is millions of disrupted users and, in Qwen's case, lost data with no clear recovery path; for Chinese regulators, the rules mark an early and unusually direct attempt to draw a hard legal line around how emotionally persuasive AI companions are allowed to become.

OpenAI Proposes Giving the US Government a 5% Stake

02.07.2026 cnbc.com

The stake would be worth roughly $42.6 billion at OpenAI's $852 billion valuation, folded into a public wealth fund modeled on Alaska's. Talks remain conceptual — and might require an act of Congress.

OpenAI has proposed handing the US government a 5% equity stake in the company, according to reporting first published by the Financial Times. At OpenAI's recent $852 billion valuation, the holding would be worth roughly $42.6 billion — one of the most unusual overtures a private technology company has ever made to its own government.

CEO Sam Altman has reportedly engaged directly with President Trump, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on the idea, after first pitching the concept to the administration in early 2025. Altman has argued that giving the public a financial interest in the company is the best way to share the upside of AI.

The mechanism would be a "Public Wealth Fund" modeled on the Alaska Permanent Fund — the $91.2 billion sovereign fund that invests the state's oil wealth and pays dividends to Alaskans. Under OpenAI's vision, America's leading AI companies would each allot around 5% of their equity to such a vehicle, giving citizens an automatic stake in AI companies and infrastructure without investing a dollar themselves. Reports name Anthropic, Google and Meta as potential participants, along with chipmakers such as NVIDIA, Micron and AMD — though none has signed on.

The proposal fits a pattern: in April 2026, OpenAI published an industrial-policy paper advocating public wealth funds, four-day work weeks, higher corporate tax rates and taxes on AI systems that replace human workers. It also lands amid sharper political pressure — Senator Bernie Sanders has floated the far more radical idea of the government taking 50% stakes in major AI companies, while Trump has called equity-sharing concepts "interesting."

For now, the discussions remain at the "conceptual" stage, with no formal agreements — and implementing such a fund could require an act of Congress. But the fact that the conversation is happening at all says much about how existential the government relationship has become for the frontier AI labs.

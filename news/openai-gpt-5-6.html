OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6 Family in Three Tiers: Sol, Terra and Luna

09.07.2026 techcrunch.com

OpenAI's most capable models yet arrive in three tiers, with Sol claiming the top coding benchmark score. A new enterprise agent, ChatGPT Work, is built to carry out whole jobs rather than just answer questions.

On July 9, 2026, OpenAI opened its most powerful system yet to the public: GPT-5.6, a new family of models shipping in three tiers named Sol, Terra and Luna, released alongside ChatGPT Work, an agent designed to complete entire jobs instead of merely answering questions.

Sol is the premium workhorse, priced at $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output tokens. Terra sits in the middle at $2.50 and $15, while Luna is the fast, budget-friendly tier at $1 and $6 — a spread clearly aimed at covering everything from frontier research workloads to high-volume production traffic.

OpenAI positions Sol as its best coding model yet. The company points to a score of 80 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index — 2.8 points above Anthropic's Fable 5 — while using less than half the output tokens and costing roughly a third less. CEO Sam Altman said Sol is "54% more token efficient" on AI coding tasks.

GPT-5.6 also puts unusual emphasis on cybersecurity, with dedicated capabilities for threat modeling, code review, patching and defensive blue teaming. That emphasis is no accident: the Trump administration had earlier sought to restrict GPT-5.6's rollout over cybersecurity concerns before this public launch.

ChatGPT Work, powered by GPT-5.6, is available on desktop, web and mobile. It pulls together context from a team's tools to turn scattered notes, drafts and ideas into finished documents, spreadsheets and presentations — and keeps projects moving while the user stays in control. GPT-5.6 is also reported to become the new preferred model in Microsoft 365 Copilot across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Chat and Cowork.

The launch lands in one of the most crowded months the frontier-model market has seen — days after Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 and ahead of Google's rebuilt Gemini 3.5 Pro — and makes clear that the race is now as much about price and agents as raw capability.

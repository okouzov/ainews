Future of Life Institute's 2026 AI Safety Index: Nobody Gets an A

07.07.2026 futureoflife.org

Nine frontier AI companies were graded on 37 safety indicators; Anthropic topped the field with a C+ while xAI, DeepSeek and Mistral failed outright — and even the leader falls well short of its own stated standards.

The Future of Life Institute has published its Summer 2026 AI Safety Index, grading nine of the world's leading AI companies on 37 indicators spanning six domains: risk assessment, current harms, safety frameworks, existential safety, governance and accountability, and information sharing. The headline finding: not one company earned better than a C+.

Anthropic took the top spot with a C+ and a numerical score of 2.66, unchanged from the institute's previous Winter 2025 index. OpenAI followed with a C (2.28), sliding down from a C+. Google DeepMind held steady at a C (2.01). Meta improved from a D to a D+ (1.32), moving up from sixth place to fourth. Z.ai and Alibaba Cloud both scored D- grades, at 0.88 and 0.87 respectively. At the bottom, xAI, DeepSeek and newcomer Mistral all received failing grades — F scores of 0.65, 0.47 and 0.33 — with xAI notably dropping from a D to an F.

The evidence behind the grades was collected through June 3, 2026, drawn from public materials such as model cards, research papers and benchmark results, supplemented by company survey responses. Seven independent reviewers — David Krueger, Sharon Li, Tegan Maharaj, Sneha Revanur, Stuart Russell, Robert Trager and Yi Zeng — assessed the evidence against absolute performance standards for each domain, rather than grading companies relative to one another.

The report's most pointed criticism concerns existential safety: no company scored better than a C- in that domain, meaning even Anthropic's industry-leading practices fall well short of what the reviewers consider adequate given the pace of capability advances. The index also found the industry broadly retreating from earlier commitments, including restrictions on military applications of frontier models — several companies are now actively pursuing defense contracts — and a softening of previously announced pause commitments tied to dangerous capability thresholds.

Reviewers repeatedly flagged a gap between companies' public safety rhetoric and their documented behavior, along with a persistent lack of quantitative risk thresholds and genuinely independent audits across the industry's safety frameworks. The geographic spread of scores undercuts any simple narrative about regulation driving safety: despite the European Union's aggressive AI regulatory push, France's Mistral scored last of all nine companies, suggesting weak safety practice is a global problem rather than one confined to less-regulated markets.

Panel members did not mince words. "AI companies' lack of progress towards credible AI safety plans is scandalous," said reviewer David Krueger. Stuart Russell offered a similar assessment: "While there is good work being done on AI safety in the industry, the capabilities race has become more extreme."

The Summer 2026 index arrives as frontier labs from Anthropic to xAI compete on ever-shorter release cycles, and its authors frame the findings as a warning that safety practices are not keeping pace — a gap the report argues becomes more dangerous, not less, the longer it persists.

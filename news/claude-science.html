Anthropic Unveils Claude Science, an AI Workbench for Researchers

30.06.2026 anthropic.com

The new workbench folds more than 60 scientific tools, databases and pipelines into one environment powered by existing Claude models. Anthropic will also back up to 50 research projects with grants of up to $30,000.

Anthropic introduced Claude Science on June 30, 2026: an AI workbench that consolidates the fragmented tooling of computational research — databases, file formats, pipelines and specialized applications — into a single environment. The company is explicit that this is not a new AI model: Claude Science runs on existing Claude models, including Claude Opus 4.8, without special access or gating.

The workbench is built around four ideas. It produces reproducible artifacts — figures and manuscripts generated alongside complete code histories, so results can be validated months later. It manages compute, from a single GPU to hundreds of processors across local machines, clusters or on-demand services. A reviewer agent checks citations and calculations and self-corrects errors as a pipeline runs. And session forking lets researchers compare different analytical approaches without losing the original thread of work.

Users interact with a generalist coordinating agent that has access to more than 60 curated skills and connectors pre-configured for genomics, single-cell analysis, proteomics, structural biology, cheminformatics and more. Integration with NVIDIA's BioNeMo toolkit adds specialized life-sciences models such as Evo 2, Boltz-2 and OpenFold3.

Early adopters are already reporting dramatic gains. Manifold Bio used the workbench for tissue-targeting drug-candidate nomination; neuroscientist Jérôme Lecoq at the Allen Institute built multi-agent literature-review pipelines that cut production time from two years to weeks; and epidemiologist Stephen Francis's team at the UCSF Brain Tumor Center accelerated germline variant analysis by roughly 90%.

Anthropic is backing the launch with a grants program supporting up to 50 "AI for Science" projects with up to $30,000 in credits each — plus up to $2,000 in compute credits from Modal — focused on biology and biomedical research. Applications are due July 15, with projects running September 1 to December 1.

Claude Science is available in beta for Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans on macOS and Linux, with administrator enablement required for organizations and a discounted Team plan for academic institutions and nonprofits.

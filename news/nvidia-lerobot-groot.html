NVIDIA Brings Isaac GR00T 1.7 and Teleop to Hugging Face's LeRobot

06.07.2026 blogs.nvidia.com

The first open, commercially viable robot foundation model meets the open-source robotics community — connecting NVIDIA's 3 million robotics developers with Hugging Face's 16 million AI builders.

NVIDIA and Hugging Face announced a collaboration that brings NVIDIA's robotics stack to LeRobot, Hugging Face's open-source robotics library — a move both companies frame as the moment open robotics gets its own foundation-model moment.

At the center is Isaac GR00T 1.7, which NVIDIA describes as its first open and commercially viable robot foundation model. It is a vision-language-action (VLA) system designed for humanoid robots that can be post-trained and deployed directly through LeRobot workflows, letting developers adapt it to new robot bodies and new tasks without starting from scratch.

Alongside the model comes Isaac Teleop, an open-source framework for capturing high-quality human demonstrations from external devices in standardized formats. Demonstration data is the fuel of modern robot learning, and Teleop is built so developers can expand and share their datasets with the community directly inside LeRobot.

The integration creates an unusually large funnel: NVIDIA claims a community of 3 million robotics developers, while Hugging Face counts 16 million AI builders. Together they get a unified pipeline — collect standardized data, train foundation models, evaluate performance and deploy, all through open workflows rather than fragmented, proprietary tooling.

More is coming: NVIDIA Cosmos 3, a frontier "world foundation model" for physical AI, is planned for future LeRobot integration. Its role will be generating synthetic robotics data and simulating scenarios where real-world data collection is too expensive or too slow — often the binding constraint in robot learning.

"Open source is how a field turns advanced research into something people can study, adapt and build on," said Hugging Face cofounder Thomas Wolf. For the fast-growing humanoid robotics field, this partnership is the clearest bet yet that openness — not walled gardens — will set the pace.

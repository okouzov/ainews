Anthropic and Blackstone Bet the Next Trillion-Dollar AI Business Is Implementation

15.07.2026 techcrunch.com

Ode, a $1.5 billion joint venture backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs, wagers that deploying AI inside real companies — not building better models — is where the next wave of value will be captured.

In May 2026, Anthropic and a consortium of private equity firms including Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs launched Ode, a $1.5 billion joint venture built around a simple wager: the biggest AI opportunity left on the table is not a smarter model, but getting existing models actually working inside real companies.

Ode was built on the acquisition of Fractional AI, an AI engineering services startup that had previously spent 11 months partnered with OpenAI before switching allegiance. Fractional's co-founders now lead the new venture: Chris Taylor as CEO and Eddie Siegel as chief technologist. "It's pretty easy to imagine this as a trillion-dollar company someday if we execute well," Taylor said.

The company currently employs about 100 engineers who work directly inside client organizations, diagnosing workflows and building custom AI systems rather than shipping standardized software. Siegel argues that this hands-on integration work, not model selection, is where the effort actually goes: "Model selection matters, but it's not where the majority of calories are spent."

Ode describes its approach as "Claude-first," reflecting Anthropic's stake in the venture, but says it is not limited to Anthropic's technology alone. Its private equity backers are expected to funnel some of their portfolio companies toward Ode as customers, though the firm says its services remain open to the broader market rather than restricted to the consortium's own holdings.

The bet reflects a widening gap between AI capability and AI adoption. Frontier labs keep shipping more powerful models, but many enterprises still struggle to translate that capability into working systems, integrated data pipelines and measurable outcomes. Ode's pitch is that closing that gap, deal by deal, is itself a business worth billions.

It is not alone in spotting the opportunity. OpenAI has its own Deployment Company pursuing a similar playbook, while consulting giants Deloitte and Accenture have built out forward-deployed engineering practices of their own, the latter through its partnership with Microsoft. The scramble suggests every major lab now sees implementation services, not just subscriptions or API access, as a durable revenue stream.

For Anthropic, the venture offers a foothold in enterprise deals it might otherwise lose to competitors' models embedded by rival deployment shops, while giving Blackstone and its peers a direct channel between their portfolio companies and frontier AI. Whether Ode reaches Taylor's trillion-dollar ambition will depend on whether "AI implementation" turns out to be a specialized skill worth a premium, or a service that commoditizes as fast as the models themselves.

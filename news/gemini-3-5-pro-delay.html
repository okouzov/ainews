Google Delays Gemini 3.5 Pro as DeepMind Faces a Talent Exodus

07.07.2026 the-agent-report.com

The flagship model missed its promised June window and now targets mid-July after a rebuild focused on token efficiency, coding and multi-step reasoning. Four senior DeepMind researchers departed within a single week.

Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro has missed the June 2026 launch window that CEO Sundar Pichai promised at Google I/O on May 19. The model has been pushed to July — reports point to July 17 — and currently sits in a limited Vertex AI preview with select enterprise customers.

According to reporting, the delay stems from three technical problems: token-efficiency issues, coding performance gaps and subpar multi-step reasoning. Fixing them has amounted to a rebuild of the model before public release, aimed squarely at the coding and agentic workloads where rivals have pulled ahead.

The markets noticed. Alphabet's stock fell roughly 5% on June 22, erasing about $225 billion in market value, and the share price slid nearly 10% from its May 13 all-time high of $402.38 to $345.29 by June 24. Analysts attributed the drop to the combined effect of the missed deadline and a bruising talent exodus.

Between June 18 and June 24, four senior DeepMind researchers left within a single week. Noam Shazeer — Gemini co-lead and co-author of the transformer architecture — departed for OpenAI just 22 months after Google rehired him in a $2.7 billion deal. John Jumper, the 2024 Nobel laureate and AlphaFold co-creator, joined Anthropic; Jonas Adler, DeepMind's Gemini coding lead, and pretraining specialist Alexander Pritzel followed him there days later.

When it does arrive, Gemini 3.5 Pro is expected to target a two-million-token context window and a "Deep Think" reasoning mode priced at $250 per month, positioning it against Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and DeepSeek V4 Flash, which have been dominating recent coding benchmarks.

The stakes could hardly be higher: July 2026 has become the most concentrated release month the frontier-model race has yet produced, and Google is now the last of the big three labs to show its hand.
